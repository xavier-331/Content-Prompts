LAMBORGHINI COUNTACH: NAMED AFTER A WORKSHOP EXCLAMATION, NOT A MARKETING MEETING
- - -

The Lamborghini Countach got its name from a workshop craftsman's exclamation, not a marketing meeting. Designer Marcello Gandini worked late nights at Bertone's studio, and a Piedmontese-speaking craftsman kept exclaiming contacc, a word that literally translates to plague or contagion but got used casually to mean something closer to wow. Gandini genuinely liked the sound of it enough to name the entire car after that reaction. The scissor doors people now associate with Lamborghini weren't originally about drama, either; Gandini had already used the same hinge design on his 1968 Alfa Romeo Carabo concept specifically to solve poor rear visibility, letting a driver lift the door and lean out to see behind the car. The Countach's chassis was so wide that conventional doors couldn't open properly in tight spaces, and the high door sills made normal exits awkward anyway. Gandini reused his own solution. Unveiled as the LP500 prototype in 1971, production finally began in 1974. Was the doors' real purpose actually function, not showmanship?
